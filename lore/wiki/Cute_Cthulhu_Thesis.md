Origin and development

The Cute Cthulhu Thesis was first articulated by 
Charlotte Fang
 on the 
Miya Black Hearted Cyber Angel Baby
 project. The concept draws its name from H.P. Lovecraft's cosmic horror entity Cthulhu, reimagined as an inescapable force that manifests in contemporary culture not as terrifying but as cute.

[
2
]

 Fang argues this is why commercial aesthetics increasingly lean toward bright, kawaii (Japanese "cute") elements, particularly in digital spaces and emerging technologies.

The concept connects to broader discussions about the evolution of capitalism into technocapitalism, where technology plays a central role in wealth accumulation and power structures. In Fang's articulation, cuteness functions as an exploitative mechanism within this system, targeting innate human psychological responses.

Key principles

The Cute Cthulhu Thesis identifies several key principles:

Cuteness as an evolved aesthetic strategy within technocapitalism that targets human psychological biases

The inevitability of kawaii and "hypercute" visuals as capitalism advances into AI-dominated markets

The role of neoteny (infantilized features) and saccharine elements in triggering dopaminergic responses

Fang specifically points to the commercial targeting of two elements: "saccharine visuals of sugar" and the "neoteny of babies" as the most efficient exploitations of human irrationality in market settings.

]

Application to Remilia projects

The thesis helps explain the aesthetic choices behind several 
Remilia Corporation
 projects, most notably the 
Milady Maker
 NFT collection, which Fang describes as 
neochibi
. According to Fang, Milady's aesthetic is "essentially an artistic embodiment of this thesis" – deliberately leveraging cute, infantilized features while subverting expectations through unexpected juxtapositions and contexts.
