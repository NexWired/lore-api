# Neochibi

*From Remilia Wiki*

Remilia Corporation's contemporary, post-anime reinterpretation of Japanese chibi aesthetics, characterized by deliberately childlike, amateurish qualities often drawn in a Lefthand style – resembling art created with a non-dominant hand or mouse.

The style serves as the visual foundation for Remilia's digital NFTs such as Milady Maker.

## Background: Chibi Conventions

Traditional chibi (ちび, colloquially meaning "small" or "short") originated as an anime and manga art style characterized by:
- Exaggerated proportions
- Oversized heads (1/3 to 1/2 of total character height)
- Simplified rounded bodies with stubby limbs
- Minimal detail

The style emerged through the 1970s-1980s, becoming codified through SD Gundam (1985) and widespread anime usage.

## Post-Anime Deconstruction

Remilia's approach operates through a **post-anime** frame, where anime aesthetics are deliberately deconstructed and reinterpreted through a more critical, self-aware lens.

This involves the deliberate use of childlike, amateurish elements that reject the polish and professionalism of mainstream anime production—embracing imperfection and naivety as aesthetic values.

## Lefthand Style

A defining characteristic of Remilia's neochibi. Artwork appears to be drawn with a non-dominant hand (or computer mouse), resulting in:

- Deliberately unrefined linework that appears shaky or hesitant
- Asymmetrical features and proportions
- Simplified details suggesting amateur execution
- Digital appearance resembling early internet art or MS Paint drawings
- **Rejection of technical polish and professional refinement**

The lefthand style democratizes artistic participation by challenging technical virtuosity as a prerequisite for validity.
